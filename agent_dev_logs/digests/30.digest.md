## 🎯 Goal

The issue body is narrow: _"Proposed at the meeting - to be checked with tools/recategorize … make sure the new algo is aligned with the pipeline."_ The PR-author clarification: this was meant to cover the corpus as a whole, with the 2010+ subset treated as the most important slice for the original request. The core task has always been verification of the categorisation pipeline, not a standalone feature request — that remains true after everything below.

## 🔧 What changed — PR #32 (merged 2026-07-26, `f6adc23`)

PR #32 merged seven commits into `test`, concentrated in `text_util.py` (+309/−5). Six independently reviewable logic changes, plus a CodeQL fix:

- `2 → z` OCR repair no longer fires inside numbers.
- Neutral/reference tokens (dates, cites, initials, sigla, dotted abbreviations, units) excluded from the valid-word ratio.
- Short lines (1–2 words) routed through a structural gate instead of language confidence.
- A character-level damage detector caps damaged lines at `Noisy`.
- A clean-reference floor can lift some `Trash` lines to `Noisy`, never the reverse.
- Extra fragment/bigram/case-mix/solitary-letter rules push obvious garbage to `Trash`.

Measured with `tools/recategorize_from_csv.py`, its own stated instrument: 61.3% → 79.0% on a 300-line blind sample, with a real recall/precision tradeoff on the hardest "needs attention" band (recall 72.2% → 46.3%, precision 30.7% → 50.0%).

## 🔁 What changed since — the follow-up chain (`b50c1f3` → `6486c72` → `641f936`)

PR #32 shipped with several tests still red. What followed was a same-day, then next-day, cross-model review cycle that treated the merge as a checkpoint, not a finish line:

- **Root cause found first.** Several `test_recategorize_parity` failures traced to one thing: `rule_lowppl_clear` short-circuiting straight to `Clear` before `is_forgiven_headline`/`check_rescues()` ever got a chance to run, on a 9-word em-dash-separated title line. The bug predated the WIP commit.
- **`b50c1f3`** fixed the immediate parity failures (495 passed) and shifted real category volume on the sample docs (`Clear` +130, `Noisy` −101, `Trash` −29 across 251 changed lines).
- **A same-day logic audit** of that commit flagged two of its changes as substantive judgment calls rather than bug fixes, not just plumbing:
  1. the six pre-existing structural override rules (rotation/wqx, vowel-less, ledger fragmentation, mid-uppercase, bigram runs, fragment tokens) had been pulled out of `determine_category()` and reimplemented as cumulative `qs -= 0.35` quality-score penalties — reversing the pure threshold-routing architecture the categoriser had been moved to earlier;
  2. a new blanket `" — " + digit` substring match was forcing any such line straight to `Noisy` — directly undercutting PR #32's own argument that its flagship em-dash fixture had simply been mislabeled, not that em-dashes-with-digits are inherently bad.
  - Cross-model consensus (multiple independent reviews) converged fast on both: restore the six rules as **named, explicit gates**, not penalties; and either narrow the em-dash matcher to the actual failure mode or just fix the fixture.
- **`6486c72`** partially answered this — restored the character-level damage backstop (`if damaged: return "Noisy"`) and tightened `is_structured_line`/`_looks_like_measurement` to reject bare single-letter units. Independent blind-sample measurement: 81.0% (243/300), statistically indistinguishable from the other calibrated commits (McNemar n.s.) — but the same review found two loose predicates still letting probe garbage (`"3 m"`, `"cuxoaid v. 12"`) through as falsely "structured."
- **`641f936`** restored the six rules as explicit gates — satisfying the architecture request — and tightened the measurement predicates further. It also introduced two regressions of its own:
  1. **A real regex bug.** The tightened `full_measurement_keywords`/`descriptor_keywords` patterns end several alternatives in a literal `.` (`rozm\.`, `pr\.`, `hl\.`) inside a single alternation wrapped in one trailing `\b`. A boundary can't exist right after a non-word character (`.`) when the next character is also non-word (a space) — so `"pr. okraje - 145"`, `"rozm. 12"`, and `"pr. dna - 7"` silently lost measurement protection whenever OCR left a space after the dot, while glued forms (`"pr.okraje"`) kept working. Restricting units to multi-character-only *also* cost roughly 3,282 lines carrying a bare metre — the single most common unit in the corpus by a wide margin (48,387 occurrences vs. 4,190 for `mm`) — a restriction one reviewer had proposed a round earlier and then explicitly withdrew after measuring the cost.
  2. `is_forgiven_headline`'s docstring and all inline step-comments were deleted outright in the same edit — not shortened, removed — with no note explaining why. The underlying logic was untouched.
  - Net corpus effect (9.1M lines): 11,955 moved, 99.95% of them demotions (`Clear→Noisy` 7,460, `Clear→Trash` 3,093, `Noisy→Trash` 1,396). Roughly a third traces to the regex defect; the rest to the newly-hardened gates. Blind-sample accuracy didn't move (still 243/300) — the corpus-scale effect is invisible at n=300.
  - One incidental finding, explicitly flagged as neither a regression in intent nor something to fix in the same pass: ~237 copies of `"Plocha: 3; sonda: XIV."`-style archaeological headers lost a protection that had only ever existed by accident (a separator token happened to match inside the Roman numeral). Logged as "needs somewhere else to land," not actioned.

Two regression tests were also added, isolating the boundary bug specifically — the existing `TestStructuredLineMeasurement` assertions all happened to pass via a different path even on the buggy code, so none of them actually pinned it. Both new tests were confirmed to fail against pre-fix `text_util.py` and pass against the fix. Full suite: 510 passed / 4 skipped / 4 deselected.

**Not in scope, by deliberate choice:** a new rule for the `"sonda: XIV."` pattern. It isn't a regression in intent, the 273-doc corpus it was observed in isn't in this repo's committed fixtures, and it isn't one of the four agreed items — writing a pattern for it now would be unverified against real data, the exact failure mode this whole review cycle has been pushing back against.

**Still pending:** items 1 and 4 exist as a reviewed local patch (`text_util.py` + `tests/test_categorization_routes.py`), not yet committed to `test`. The corpus-scale prediction (recovering ~3,282 `m`-unit lines, fixing the dot-boundary cases) hasn't been re-run against the actual 273-document corpus, which lives only on K4TEL's machine.

## 🧭 What the current `test` branch docs say

Unchanged from before, and still accurate post-`641f936`:

- `forgiven` is computed after the earlier gates, so it can only rescue lines that would otherwise fall to `Trash` late in the pipeline.
- The (now explicit-gate) structural rules for rotated/wqx-heavy, vowelless, fragmented, and mid-uppercase lines sit alongside `check_rescues()`, which can still rescue some lines from `Trash` to `Noisy`.
- The main score-band routing is still threshold-based, with `lm_confident_czech` and `rule_lowppl_clear` acting as important exceptions — and the source of the parity bug fixed in `b50c1f3`.
- `categorize_line()` clamps the stored `quality_score` to the band implied by the final label.
- `apply_document_postprocessing()` is still part of the production path and is confirmed reused, byte-for-byte, by `tools/recategorize_from_csv.py`, `classify_TEXT.py`, `test_smoke.py`, and `test_rotation_regression.py` — all four import directly from `text_util.py`, no parallel implementation exists anywhere.

## ⚠️ What still looks suspicious

The scope-drift concern from the original digest is now sharper, not resolved: an actual architecture regression (penalties standing in for gates) and a live regex bug both shipped inside PR #32's *own* follow-up commits, and neither was caught by the committed test suite — both were found by hand, through cross-model corpus-scale measurement, not by CI. That the six-rule question is now settled (gates, not penalties) is progress, but it's also a reminder that this issue's "alignment check" scope kept absorbing real policy decisions along the way.

Two things still deserve scrutiny, unchanged from before:

- The short-line logic is a broad behavioral change — archival records are full of short catalogue-style lines.
- The clean-reference floor and extra fragment rules are policy decisions, not just plumbing fixes.

Neither was part of the 4-item action plan and neither has been re-audited in this pass.
